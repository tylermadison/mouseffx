<Section id="overview">

Sixteen glowing ropes hang from the cursor. Each rope is a chain of points that keep a fixed distance from their neighbours. Gravity pulls each rope in its own direction, so the bundle opens like a sea anemone. A wind that changes with time moves the rope ends. When you move the cursor fast, the ropes whip, and the fast tips throw sparks.

The effect is plain canvas 2D. There is no physics library and no WebGL. All state is in typed arrays: four `Float32Array` for each rope (position and previous position, `x` and `y`).

The element count in the statistics is the total number of rope points. It changes between page loads, because each rope gets a random length: 16 ropes of 29 to 38 points give 464 to 608 points on a desktop viewport. A viewport narrower than 800 px gets 9 ropes of 17 to 26 points.

</Section>

<Section id="interaction">

- **Move.** Point 0 of each rope is pinned to the cursor. The rope has no velocity variable, so a fast cursor move becomes a large displacement of point 0, and the constraint solver passes that displacement down the chain. This is the whip.
- **Move fast.** When a rope tip moves faster than 900 px/s, it emits sparks that keep 35% of the tip velocity.
- **Hold the button.** The ropes become electrified: gravity goes to zero, the drag becomes much stronger, a large random force shakes each point on each frame, and the ropes are drawn white.
- **Idle.** After 2.5 s with no input, the shared pointer goes into autopilot and moves on a slow path. The ropes follow it, so the background does not freeze.

The module stores the `reduced` flag from `init`, but `update` does not read it. Reduced motion does not slow this effect.

</Section>

<Section id="model">

### Verlet integration

A point has a position `x` and a previous position `px`. The velocity is not stored. It is the difference between the two, and the step is:

<Formula caption="Position Verlet with drag. a is the acceleration, dt the time step.">{`v      = (x − px) · drag
px     = x
x      = x + v + a · dt²`}</Formula>

This form is the reason that the effect is stable with a simple constraint solver. When a constraint moves a point, the velocity of the next frame changes with it, and no separate velocity correction is necessary.

One property is important: `v` is a displacement for each frame, not for each second, and `drag` is applied one time for each frame. The motion thus depends a little on the frame rate. `dt` is limited to 1/30 s, and on a usual 60 Hz display the result is the tuned one.

### Forces

Each point `i` of a rope with `n` points gets this acceleration:

<Formula caption="The factor i / n makes the wind zero at the anchor and full at the tip.">{`a.x = wind(t) · (i / n) + G · cos(gdir)
a.y = wave(t, i) · (i / n) + G · sin(gdir)

wind(t)    = 160 · sin(t · freq + phase) + 80 · sin(0.37 · t + r)
wave(t, i) =  90 · sin(1.3 · t + phase + 0.2 · i)`}</Formula>

- `G = 380` px/s² is gravity. It does not point straight down. Each rope `r` has its own direction `gdir = π/2 + ((r + 0.5) / R − 0.5) · 2.4`. For 16 ropes this is a fan of approximately ±64° around straight down (the comment in the source rounds this to ±70°).
- `wind` is horizontal. It is the sum of a fast term with a random frequency and phase for each rope, and a slow term that all ropes share apart from an offset of `r`.
- `wave` is vertical. The term `0.2 · i` shifts the phase along the rope, so the wave travels from the anchor to the tip.

### Distance constraints

After the integration, neighbour points are no longer at the rest distance `L`. The solver goes along the chain and moves each pair back:

<Formula caption="d is the current distance between points i and i + 1.">{`diff = (d − L) / d
first segment :  x[1]   −= Δ · diff            (the anchor does not move)
other segments:  x[i]   += Δ · diff · 0.5
                 x[i+1] −= Δ · diff · 0.5`}</Formula>

One pass does not satisfy all constraints, because the correction of one segment disturbs the segment before it. The module runs 4 passes on each frame. This is Gauss-Seidel relaxation. With 4 passes the ropes are not fully rigid: they stretch a little when you whip them, and that elastic look is intended.

</Section>

<Section id="pipeline">

<Pipeline>
  <Step title="Fade the canvas" reads="previous frame" writes="canvas">
    A rectangle in the background colour is drawn with alpha `1 − 0.78^(60 · dt)`. This keeps 78% of the old image for each 1/60 s at all frame rates, and it makes the light trails.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pin the anchor" reads="pointer.x, pointer.y" writes="x[0], px[0]">
    Point 0 goes to the cursor plus an offset of 6 px on a circle that turns at 0.8 rad/s. Each rope has its own angle on that circle, so the ropes do not start from one pixel.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Integrate" reads="x, px" writes="x, px">
    Points 1 to n − 1 do the Verlet step with gravity, wind, and wave.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Relax the constraints" reads="x" writes="x">
    Four passes along the chain.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Emit sparks" reads="tip x, px" writes="spark pool">
    Tip speed is `(x − px) / dt`. Above 900 px/s, a spark is made with a probability of 0.6 on that frame.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Draw the rope" reads="x" writes="canvas">
    Two strokes of one smooth path with additive blend (`lighter`): a wide glow and a thin bright core.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Update and draw the sparks" reads="spark pool" writes="spark pool, canvas">
    All 900 slots are examined. Live sparks fall with 600 px/s², lose 2% of their velocity for each frame, and are drawn as 2 × 2 px squares.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Draw the anchor glow" reads="pointer.x, pointer.y" writes="canvas">
    A radial gradient of 40 px radius at the cursor.
  </Step>
</Pipeline>

Steps 2 to 6 run one time for each rope.

</Section>

### Rope setup

Each rope gets its own length, segment length, colour, wind phase and frequency, line width, and gravity direction. All points start in a straight line below the cursor, with `px = x`, so the start velocity is zero.

### Integration

`drag` and `G` are set one time for each frame from the button state. Note that the force is multiplied by `dt2` (dt²), and the velocity term is not multiplied by a time value. The last line pins point 0, and it also sets `px[0]`, so the anchor has no velocity of its own.

<Excerpt file="effects/tendrils.js" region="integrate" page="tendrils" />

### Constraint relaxation

`|| 1e-4` prevents a division by zero when two points are at the same position. The first segment gives the full correction to point 1. All other segments divide it between the two points.

<Excerpt file="effects/tendrils.js" region="constraints" page="tendrils" />

### Sparks from the tip

The tip velocity is converted to px/s first, so that the 900 px/s limit does not depend on the frame rate.

<Excerpt file="effects/tendrils.js" region="tip-sparks" page="tendrils" />

The pool is a ring buffer of 900 slots. A new spark overwrites the oldest slot, so there is no allocation in the frame loop:

### Drawing

The points are the control points of quadratic curves, and the curves end at the midpoints between the points. This gives a smooth line through a chain of straight segments with no spline library.

<Section id="performance">

The cost is on the CPU, and it has two parts. The physics is small: about 540 points × 4 constraint passes is approximately 2,200 square roots for each frame. The larger part is canvas drawing: 32 stroked paths with round caps and additive blend, and one full-screen fill for the fade.

Limits that the module applies:

- The device pixel ratio is limited to 2.
- The canvas is opaque (`alpha: false`), so the browser does not blend it with the page below it.
- The spark loop has no allocation. It skips dead slots with one comparison.
- The rope count and length go down on viewports narrower than 800 px.
